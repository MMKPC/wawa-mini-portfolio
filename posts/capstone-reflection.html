From WAWA Mini to a defended capstone

How each course built AYO Mini, what was hard, and where the work goes next.

By Matthew Mitchell · November 22, 2026 · MMKPC Studios

On November 20, 2026, I defended AYO Mini and the committee passed it. This post closes the capstone: what I built, how each class fed into it, and what I would tell the next student.

Reflection video

Project demo: the Rafy pages

AYO Mini retrieves original passages from local game design notes. For the final build I extended it to a short film, Rafy: pick any frame and AYO Mini returns the shot's note and the documented cue for that moment, with its source. The second page puts each scene note beside the exact still.

How each course contributed

COS550 · Advanced Software Engineering

July 2026

This is where the project started. I built WAWA Mini, a Python command-line tool that reads a game design document, summarizes each section with a local Phi-3 model and assembles a production map. The design document, the Git workflow and the unit tests from this course are the base the capstone grew from. Lesson: a written design and real tests make the next course easier, because you are extending something instead of starting over.

COS570 · Advanced AI

August 2026

Five projects in four weeks: a smart-city traffic and energy simulation, a chatbot, face aging, generative language and autonomous drones, plus an IEEE signature article and weekly reflections. The pace taught me to measure first and claim only what the numbers show. That habit is why my thesis reports what the fixture shows and not more.

COS580 · Machine Learning

September 2026

Regression, clustering and classification with pandas and scikit-learn. This is where evaluation metrics became a habit. AYO Mini is scored with Recall@5 and MRR@5 on a labeled fixture because of this course.

COS660 · Capstone: Development I

September – October 2026

WAWA Mini became AYO Mini. I added a persistent SQLite FTS5 keyword index, then local Nomic embeddings for semantic search, and shipped a milestone each week with Asana tracking, an advisor and my first thesis draft.

COS680 · Deployment and Professional Presentation

October – November 2026

Hybrid search with reciprocal rank fusion, the Rafy scene-notes pages on AYO Mini 0.5.1, the final thesis, my resume and the defense. On the October 23 fixture, OR/BM25 reached 0.980 macro Recall@5 and 1.000 MRR@5 with a 0.96 ms median ranking time.

What was hard, and what would have helped

Finding an advisor. My first choice was at capacity, and my advisor, Dr. Moody Amakobe, was confirmed through Dr. Leis in week two. An advisor assigned at the start of Development I would have saved a week of work done without one.

Evaluating honestly. My fixture uses synthetic notes and labels without independent judges, so it cannot prove which method wins on real studio work. More curriculum time on evaluation design, such as held-out sets and independent labeling, would help every data science capstone.

Semantic search is slower. Dense and hybrid search took about a second per query against under a millisecond for keyword search. Knowing that trade-off early shaped what the tool does by default.

The program

The most useful part was continuity. I carried one project through several courses, so each class added a layer instead of a throwaway assignment. Feedback came with each graded milestone and was specific; the design-document note to show what my advisor changed is one I acted on. The improvement I would suggest is earlier advisor matching, and a GitHub workflow that replaces the retired GitHub Classroom link.

How I build software changed: smaller pieces, measured before I claim anything. How I evaluate it changed too. A retrieved passage is only useful if a person checks the original before a draft becomes a rule. This tool is not autonomous, and it should not be.

Next, I am building my own studio, with AYO Mini as one of its tools, and testing it on permission-cleared production notes. The committee also suggested a PhD, and I am taking that seriously.

For students starting the capstone

Lock in your advisor in week one. Keep one project alive across courses. Measure before you claim, and keep your evidence (commits, screenshots, test runs) as you go, not the night before.
